National bulletins vs the ASAP trigger — September 2026

On 4 September 2026 the framework's observational trigger was provisionally not activated for the 3rd dekad of August, and partners were asked to share any contrary information. Three Burkinabè documents came back: the national meteorological agency's (ANAM) July drought bulletin, ANAM's rainfall situation at 31 August, and the results of the SAP-GTP joint field mission of late July. This page compares them with the JRC ASAP alerts behind the trigger and asks whether any of them could serve as a trigger.

Bottom line. The three documents agree with ASAP that the four provinces are having a worse-than-normal season: late onset, seasonal rainfall 10–50 % below normal, dry spells of 7–10 days, low dam levels, delayed sowing. None of them shows the crop or pasture biomass failure that Trigger 2 requires, and none contradicts the non-activation. As they stand they are a good verification layer for the ASAP decision, but not usable as a formal trigger: no province-level values, no historical archive to backtest against, and publication lags of one to two months.

Résumé. Les trois documents nationaux (bulletin de suivi sécheresse ANAM de juillet, situation pluviométrique ANAM au 31 août, résultats de la mission conjointe SAP-GTP) confirment une saison pire que la normale dans les provinces de Loroum, Oudalan, Séno et Yagha : installation tardive, cumuls déficitaires, séquences sèches, faibles taux de remplissage des barrages. Aucun ne met en évidence le déficit de biomasse (cultures ou pâturages) exigé par le déclencheur 2, et aucun ne contredit la non-activation. Ils sont utiles pour la vérification, mais ne peuvent pas servir de déclencheur formel en l'état.

1 · The trigger and where it stands #

The 2026 framework (document of 17 April 2026) has two independent triggers over four provinces — Loroum (Yaadga region) and Oudalan, Séno, Yagha (Liptako region). Trigger 1, the April SEAS5 seasonal forecast, did not activate. Trigger 2 is observational:

Trigger 2. Activated if the JRC ASAP platform issues a level-3 alert (water balance and biomass) for crop or rangeland areas in at least two of the four provinces in any dekad from the 3rd dekad of July to the 2nd dekad of September inclusive (dekads 21–26).

Every province has sat at level 1, for both cropland and rangeland, in every dekad since the season started (Figure 1). The ASAP warning text is the same everywhere: at least 25 % of the active area has a water-balance deficit (in Loroum also a rainfall SPI-3 deficit), while at least 25 % of the active area shows a positive vegetation anomaly. A level-3 alert needs a vegetation deficit and a meteorological deficit to co-occur, so ASAP cannot escalate while greenness is at or above normal.

2 · What the national documents say about the AOI #

Burkina Faso's regions were renamed in 2025. In the documents, the AOI appears as Liptako (Oudalan, Séno, Yagha) and the western part of Yaadga (Loroum). Soum, now its own region, borders the AOI to the west and is often named alongside it; it is not in the framework.

3 · How this compares with ASAP #

Where they agree

  • Rainfall deficit. ASAP's level-1 warnings are driven by the water-balance index (WSI) and, in Loroum, SPI-3. ANAM's SPI-1 and SPI-3 maps and the station totals show the same thing: a 10–30 % seasonal deficit at the AOI stations, worst in the north.
  • Late, difficult start. The mission's sowing delays and the ANAM onset map (July onset across Liptako and Soum) match ASAP's season only becoming active in the 3rd dekad of June to 1st dekad of July.
  • No biomass failure. ANAM's VHI map puts vegetation stress in the centre and west of the country, not in the AOI. ASAP goes further and reports a positive vegetation anomaly on at least 25 % of the active area in every province. Both say the season is bad on rainfall and not (yet) bad on vegetation.

The one tension

The joint mission described vegetation growth as delayed in Liptako, with sparse vegetation and bare soil across Soum, Liptako and Yaadga on 20 July, while ASAP reported a positive greenness anomaly in the same provinces from the 1st dekad of July. These are not necessarily contradictory: ASAP's "active area" excludes pixels where the season has not started, so a late onset can shrink the area ASAP judges rather than flag it, and a positive anomaly on the pixels that did start is compatible with bare soil elsewhere. It is worth checking directly on the ASAP explorer, by looking at the FPAR time series and the active-area fraction for Séno and Yagha, before the September dekads are assessed.

Where 2026 sits in the record

Figure 2 shows, for every year since 2001, how many of the four provinces were at level 3 or higher in each dekad of the trigger window. The years that would have activated Trigger 2 are 2002, 2004, 2006, 2009 and 2011 — the same five as in the framework document. A 2026 profile of level 1 everywhere resembles 2001, 2014 or 2019, none of which activated.

4 · Could these documents be used as a trigger? #

A trigger has to be an objective threshold on an indicator that is available for the provinces in question, published in time to act, and backtestable over the historical record against both an impact record and an observational one, so that a return period can be reported. 5 · Recommendations #

  1. Keep ASAP as Trigger 2 for this season. Nothing in the documents argues for overriding it, and the protocol has no provision for doing so.
  2. Formalize a verification checklist for each Trigger 2 decision: the latest ANAM SPI-3 map, the ANAM station totals versus normal for the AOI and neighbouring stations, and the most recent SAP-GTP or CNSA assessment. Record what each says alongside the ASAP result in the notification.
  3. Check the FPAR time series and active-area fraction for Séno and Yagha on the ASAP explorer, to resolve the delayed-vegetation versus positive-anomaly question before the September dekads.
  4. Ask ANAM for historical station rainfall or their gridded SPI for 2001–2025. With that record a rainfall-only observational trigger (for example SPI-3 for July–September below −1 in at least two provinces) could be tested against the target years and given a return period in a future revision. This is a data-sharing request; the bulletins themselves cannot support that analysis.

Watch-point for the rest of the season. ANAM's Aug–Oct outlook flags long end-of-season dry spells (more than 9 days) over Liptako and Yaadga, which would hit crops sown in July at grain filling in late September and October. The Trigger 2 window closes with the 2nd dekad of September, so that risk falls outside the framework as designed. It is worth naming in the final monitoring note so that the 2027 revision can consider it.

6 · Next monitoring steps #

  • 1st dekad of September (dekad 25): ASAP publishes around 11–13 September.
  • 2nd dekad of September (dekad 26): the last dekad in the window, published around 21–23 September. If neither reaches level 3 in two provinces, Trigger 2 closes without activation for 2026.
